## Handpicked Techniques

### Philosophy

Two idea that I like to facilitate are 

- ``Make your diagram a work of art,'' and 
- ``Label everything you know.'' 

In essence, your diagrams that you draw should be almost perfectly accurate and labeled. You want to be able to draw conclusions from your diagrams with ease.

### Angle Chasing

In general, I like to say ``When you don't know what to do, just angle chase!'' You will be awfully surprised as to how well this goes. Unfortunately, it isn't so simple all the time. Here is a sample idea:
>
  \textbf{Identify any pairs of equal angles in the following setup.} \\
  Consider a triangle $ABC$ and there exists a point $P$ such that $AP = BP = CP$.
>

Observations. The above is a simple example. Note that equal sides means equal angles. For instance, $\triangle APB$ is isosceles with $AP = BP$. It therefore follows that $\angle PAB = \angle PBA$. Likewise, $\angle PBC = \angle PCB$ and $\angle PCA = \angle PAC$.

In this instance, it is important to notice equal sides implies equal angles. Now, not every problem is like this. You may have to notice some different ideas to obtain angle relationships. But, it all revolves around the key idea of using angles to establish relationships which will ultimately lead to fresh conclusions.

\begin{exercise}
\textbf{Example 1} (American Mathematics Competition)\textbf{.} Let $ABCD$ be a square and $E$ be a point outside square $ABCD$ such that $ABE$ is an equilateral triangle. Compute the measure of $\angle AED$, in degrees.
\end{exercise}

Consider triangle $AED$: note that as $AD = AB$ due to square $ABCD$ and $AB = AE$ due to equilateral triangle $ABE$. Hence, it follows that $AE = AD$ and $\triangle ADE$ is isosceles. Angle chasing,
\begin{align*}
    \angle DAE = \angle DAB + \angle BAE = 150^{\circ}.
\end{align*}
It therefore follows that $\angle AED = \dfrac{180 - \angle AED}{2} = 15^{\circ}$.

### Use Power of a Point

Translate secant and chord configurations into product equalities.

## Key Ideas

- If a quadrilateral is cyclic, opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- Power of a point: for chords through a point, $PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD$.
- Similar triangles yield proportional sides and equal angles.

## Worked Example

In a circle, chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at $P$. If $PA=3$, $PB=5$, and
$PC=2$, then $PD=\frac{PA\cdot PB}{PC}=\frac{15}{2}$.

## More Examples

### Example 1: Cyclic Angle

If $ABCD$ is cyclic and $\angle A=70^\circ$, find $\angle C$.

$110^\circ$.

### Example 2: Similar Triangles

If $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ and $AB/DE=3/2$, find the area ratio.

The area ratio is $(3/2)^2=9/4$.

### Example 3: Power of a Point

From external point $P$, a secant meets the circle at $A,B$ with $PA=4$ and
$PB=9$. Find the tangent length.

$PT^2=4\cdot 9=36$, so $PT=6$.

## Strategy Checklist

- Mark angles and look for cyclic quadrilaterals.
- Seek similar triangles before computing lengths.
- Translate circle configurations into power-of-point equations.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assuming cyclicity without proof.
- Using a similarity ratio for areas without squaring.
- Confusing tangent and secant lengths.
